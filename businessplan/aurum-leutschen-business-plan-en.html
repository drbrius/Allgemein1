Chapter 02

Vision & The Three Pillars

Switzerland is the global centre of gold refining: a substantial share of the world's gold passes through Swiss refineries, and four of the world's largest refiners are domiciled here. At the same time, demand for physical, segregated custody outside the banking system is growing structurally — driven by family offices, UHNW individuals, bullion dealers and fund platforms. Between the industrial mega-refineries in Ticino and Neuchâtel and small regional assay houses lies a gap: a premium operator in Greater Zurich combining refining, assaying, minting and institutional-scale vaulting at a single address.

I

The Refinery

Melting, sampling, fire assay and ICP analytics; refining to 999.9 fine via aqua regia and Wohlwill electrolysis; casting and minting of bars from 1 g to 12.5 kg; hallmarking under Swiss precious-metals law. Build-out capacity 100 tonnes of fine gold per annum, LBMA Good Delivery as mid-term objective.

II

The Vault

Three fully underground levels (≈ 2,100 m²) engineered to the highest EN 1143-1 resistance grades: client suites and 600 safe-deposit boxes on Level −1, segregated private vault rooms on Level −2, and a high-density bulk vault for more than 1,000 tonnes of palletised Good Delivery bars on Level −3.

III

The Security House

A 24/7, permanently double-staffed security command at the single point of entry: vehicle interlock, armoured-transport dock, visitor identification, redundant alarm routing to a certified receiving centre and the Schwyz cantonal police. Defence in depth across six security zones.

Positioning

Aurum Leutschen positions itself as «The Vault of Zurich's Gold Coast» — the discreet Swiss address where gold is refined, certified, insured and safeguarded across its entire life cycle: acquisition, assay, refining, minting, certification, custody and delivery. Metal received for refining flows directly into the vault without ever leaving the secured perimeter; vaulted metal can be re-refined, re-cast and re-certified in-house — a closed loop no bank and no standalone warehouse can offer.

Mid-term objectives: accreditation as a Swiss federally licensed melter and trade assayer, ISO/IEC 17025 laboratory accreditation, ISO 27001 for information security — and, at build-out, admission to the LBMA Good Delivery List.

Chapter 04

Architecture — A Gallery, Not a Fortress

The design reference is the FORMS Gallery in Freienbach: a former industrial hall transformed, with a reduced material palette and precise detailing, into a premium design destination. Aurum Leutschen translates that attitude into a new build — the security is absolute, and invisible.

Design principles

  • One calm, monolithic volume. A single hall with an integrated two-storey head building; precise joint layout, deep reveals, no “bunker look” — the strongest rooms in the canton hide beneath a serene façade.
  • The head building as a gallery. Reception, client lounge, private viewing suites and a bar-product showroom finished to gallery standard — trust, rendered in space.
  • Industrial precision inside. Column-free refinery hall on an 8-metre grid, 5-tonne crane runway, services routed in floor channels, north-facing glazed sheds for daylight.
  • Quiet sustainability. Full-roof photovoltaics (≈ 400 kWp), furnace waste-heat recovery, rainwater retention, Minergie standard for the head building.

Chapter 06

Refinery Operations

Capacity stages

StagePeriodFine gold p.a.Focus
Stage 1Operating years 1–225 – 40 trecycling / legacy gold, semi-finished goods, bar & coin conversion
Stage 2Operating years 3–455 – 70 t+ bank material, industrial residues, electronics concentrates
Stage 3From year 5up to 100 t+ doré from certified mines; LBMA Good Delivery capability

Process chain

  1. Intake & identification. Reception through the goods interlock, certified weighing, unbroken chain of custody, KYC and provenance screening before acceptance.
  2. Melting & sampling. Induction furnaces (2 × 150 kg, 1 × 50 kg), homogenisation, standards-compliant sampling.
  3. Assay. Fire assay (cupellation) as the reference method, supported by XRF and ICP-OES; laboratory accreditation to ISO/IEC 17025 targeted; 48-hour assay commitment.
  4. Refining. Wet-chemical refining (aqua regia) for high-grade feed and Wohlwill electrolysis for 999.9 fine gold; Moebius silver electrolysis as a side line; recovery of PGM values.
  5. Casting & minting. Cast and minted bars from 1 g to 1 kg, 12.5 kg standard bars, granules for industry; hallmarking, serial numbers, certificates, sealed packaging.
  6. Transfer. Direct transfer into the vault via the internal valuables lock — or dispatch through the armoured-transport dock.

People

Build-up from ≈ 34 FTE in year one to ≈ 60 FTE at full operation: metallurgists and refinery operators, assayers and laboratory staff, vault and security officers, client relationship managers, compliance and administration. Recruiting draws on the Zurich/Zug labour market; key hires (head of refining, head of compliance, security director) are committed before financial close.

Environment, Energy & Safety

  • Air. Multi-stage off-gas treatment (scrubbers, NOx abatement, filtration) compliant with the Swiss Air Pollution Control Ordinance; continuous emissions monitoring.
  • Water. Closed process loops; neutralisation and precipitation plant; heavy-metal limits per the Water Protection Ordinance; indirect-discharge permit.
  • Chemicals. Dedicated acid store (hazmat room, containment, gas detection) per chemical and SUVA regulations; Major Accidents Ordinance screening with the cantonal environment office.
  • Noise. Sensitivity level IV at the site; dominant sources (re-coolers, extraction) nonetheless attenuated to state of the art in deference to the residential zones across the street.
  • Energy. ≈ 1.2 MW connected load (furnaces, electrolysis); ≈ 400 kWp rooftop PV; furnace waste-heat recovery for space and water heating; UPS and generator backing for security and critical process systems.
  • Occupational safety. SUVA sector solution, explosion-protection zoning, PPE regime, occupational-health monitoring for recycling feed (lead, mercury).
  • Excavation. The contaminated-sites entry (29_B130) requires a disposal concept for excavated material — coordinated with the cantonal environment office; premium priced into CAPEX.
The environmental case is a commercial asset: documented emissions discipline, renewable self-supply and a transparent responsible-sourcing regime are prerequisites for LBMA accreditation — and for the institutional custody clientele next door.

Digital backbone

  • Client portal: holdings, bar lists, audit certificates, insurance confirmations in real time

Business Model & Revenue Engines

EngineMechanicsShare of Y5 revenue
1 · Toll refining & conversionFee per gram by material class (legacy/scrap higher, doré lower); blended planning assumption CHF 0.30 per gram of fine gold≈ 43%
2 · Custody & vault servicesStorage fee, blended ≈ 0.20% p.a. of custodied value, plus handling, audits, boxes and suites≈ 51%
3 · Trading & productsBid/offer margin on branded bars, legacy-gold purchase, by-metal revenues (Ag, PGM)≈ 6%

Value drivers

  • Loop lock-in. Refining clients become custody clients and vice versa; switching costs compound with every stored bar list and every audit trail.
  • Recurring, price-geared revenue. Custody is subscription income with > 90% retention that scales with the gold price — the three-level vault turns storage capacity into the dominant earnings engine.
  • Hard-asset backing. Lakeside-region industrial land plus a special-purpose building create a tangible value floor beneath the operating case.
  • Capacity ladder. Refining scales through shift work before capital is spent; vault levels are fitted out in phases as custody mandates land.
Why the bigger vault changes the equation. With more than 1,200 tonnes of capacity across three levels, custody — not refining — becomes the principal profit pool at maturity: recurring, insured, collateral-grade revenue from an asset that cannot be replicated without years of permitting and construction.
